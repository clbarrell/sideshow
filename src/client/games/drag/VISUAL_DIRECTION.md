# Drag visual direction

Drag looks like a live ink argument on a pale printmaker's cutting mat. The world is warm paper (`#f3ead5`) with a sparse navy grid; the safety frame is a heavy charcoal crop mark, and the outer warning margin washes toward vermilion without hiding any player. The camera never zooms or shakes. The moving grid and one fat pull arrow make camera movement read as a rule rather than an accident.

Players are hand-cut ink blobs in their seat colour. Each keeps a numbered white seat stamp and a different interior mark, so colour is reinforcement rather than the only identity. Names sit in dark, high-contrast tabs above bodies at every size. Growth swells the silhouette within a narrow range; lunging squashes, then stretches it. Reduced motion keeps the same poses but removes wobble, trails, pulses and incidental particles.

Food appears as two separated pools of small black droplets ringed in mint. Preview rings arrive before the droplets become edible, making the route choice readable. The HUD is limited to the 90-second clock, the current pull direction and a compact top-three score strip. Danger owns the hierarchy: a thick red ring, a whole-number countdown beside the threatened blob, and a matching phone state.

The launch runway begins immediately with harmless movement on the complete scene. At reset, the paper receives a broad ink-wipe, bodies return to equal size, then three block-number beats end in an unmistakable `GO!`. The outer edge is a visible retention rim: it holds the complete blob and countdown on screen while the player steers back through the inset danger line. The finish lands as one oversized ink stamp before the shell takes over.
