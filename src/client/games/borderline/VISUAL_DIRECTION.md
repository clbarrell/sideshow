# Borderline visual direction

**Idea:** a campaign atlas assembled from thick painted paper on a midnight-blue chart table. It should feel tactile and political, while every live rule remains readable across a room.

- **Palette:** midnight navy sea (`#082A3B`), warm map paper (`#F5E5C6`), near-black ink (`#17242A`) and score-stamp gold (`#F2C66D`). Live land uses the ten established seat colours; names, emblems and stable seat order carry identity beyond colour.
- **Map:** one broad 12/15/18/24-province continent, sized for the launch roster and built from shared irregular borders. Cream cut edges, layered coastal shadow, restrained paper marks and permanent port seals provide depth. Province numbers and owner marks outrank decorative cartography.
- **Type:** local Alfa Slab One, with Georgia fallback, for atlas display numerals and the existing system stack for compact instructions. Strong weight, short lines and tabular timers hold up on a washed-out projector.
- **Motion:** all orders flip together. Invasions draw from permanent port seals, guard rings stamp in place, contested land flashes once, and scores bank as one beat. Reduced motion lands the same information as static arrows and rings.
- **Phone:** warm paper field, dark ink, two explicit modes, a stable 4 × 3, 5 × 3, 6 × 3 or 6 × 4 province pad matching the launch atlas, three physical-looking force tokens and one settled committed state with a smaller Edit action. It fits a 375 × 667 portrait viewport without scrolling.
- **Audio:** four optional local cues—commit, reveal, batched capture and score—leave the room free for negotiation. Missing samples fall back quietly and never delay play.
