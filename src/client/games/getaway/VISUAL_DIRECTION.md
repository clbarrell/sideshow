# Getaway visual and audio direction

The Toybox Caper concept is the production target: a tactile miniature bank courtyard photographed from a high three-quarter angle, with warm limestone, clipped hedges, modeled getaway vans, a chunky central vault, masked toy crooks and saturated gold bags. Soft upper-left light and compact contact shadows give the playfield depth while charcoal details, crimson triangles and cobalt circles preserve projector contrast.

The renderer combines repository-local WebP art with native Canvas state. A 1600×900 courtyard backplate supplies the bank façade, paving and perimeter detail. Transparent vault, hedge, van, bag and four-direction crook assets share the same perspective and lighting. They decode once and remain cached; the complete native Canvas fallback keeps the game playable when images are still loading or fail. Crooks and cover props sort by foot depth, while labels, team marks, shove arcs, protection, bag counts and scoring stay native for perfect scaling and immediate state changes. No CSS or Canvas filters allocate work in the animation loop.

Players retain their seat colour in a foot ring and always carry a one-based seat badge plus their name. Crew identity is a second channel: Crimson robbers and their left van use triangles; Cobalt robbers and their right van use circles. A protection ring, shove-ready arc, wind-up cone, contact pose, sideways spill, wobbling physical bag stack, bank-to-van bag flight and moving vault shutter make every critical rule visible. Motion is short and bounded: shove anticipation/contact/recovery, spill arcs, pickup pops, deposits and result confetti. Reduced motion keeps the state changes while removing decorative travel, bobbing, stack wobble and pulse expansion.

The mix uses eight committed, locally generated ElevenLabs v2 effects: countdown, GO, bag rustle, shove, spill, deposit, vault closure and finish. The samples stay dry and short so players can shout plans over them; the game has no music. Host cues describe shared action once, while phones play only cues relevant to their robber. The shared persistent mute and volume controls govern both surfaces. Procedural fallbacks preserve action timing if local files fail, and phone haptics reinforce confirmed pickups, hits, protection and deposits where available. Every cue retains a visible equivalent.
