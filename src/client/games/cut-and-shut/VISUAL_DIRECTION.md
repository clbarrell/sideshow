# Cut & Shut visual direction

**Idea:** a crooked council planning table that behaves like a mechanical pop-up book. The public board is charming, legible and slightly untrustworthy.

- **Palette:** dusty-lilac void (`#6F607D`) and slab (`#B8A7C8`), acid-yellow live roads/stitches (`#F2F53D`), oxblood contracts/failures (`#651C2A`), paper cream (`#FFF3D1`) and near-black ink (`#17131C`). Seat colours remain small identity chips, not semantic state.
- **Shape:** twelve thick isometric slabs with black undersides and numbered enamel seam plates. Roads use three unmistakable glyphs — `━` straight, `┗` bend, `╋` junction — repeated on projector and phone.
- **Type:** local heavy system sans with deliberately uneven baseline/rotation for display words. Utility copy stays upright and compact. No network font dependency.
- **Texture:** code-native speckles, hatch marks and offset ink shadows; no bitmap has a distinct gameplay job in this geometry-first build.
- **Hierarchy:** phase and clock first, board second, six courier labels third, public deal/stitch rail last. Contracts use oxblood only on the owning phone.
- **Motion:** folds lift alternate slabs on a 650ms ease, then settle; couriers advance on exactly six 650ms beats; accepted stitch cards pop once; failures split into no more than six fragments per courier.
- **Reduced motion:** board positions and courier cells snap at beat boundaries, fragments become a static tipped courier marker, and no shake or flashing is used. Timing, labels and colour still carry every state.
