# Cut & Shut visual direction

**Idea:** a crooked council planning table crossed with a mechanical pop-up book. It should read as one physical city the room can repair together.

- **Palette:** dusty-lilac void (`#6F607D`) and slabs (`#B8A7C8`), acid-yellow shared status (`#F2F53D`), oxblood failure (`#651C2A`), paper cream (`#FFF3D1`) and near-black ink (`#17131C`). A player's seat colour outlines their projector tile and anchors their phone.
- **Shape:** twelve thick isometric slabs with heavy ink edges. The road itself is the diagram: clear straight, bend, or junction arms with no numbered worksite badges.
- **Couriers:** the local four-direction courier atlas supplies full character sprites. Their feet anchor to road centers and a small ground arrow preserves direction at room distance. No courier codes or labels appear.
- **Type:** heavy local system sans, upright and direct. The goal, phase, timer, and one shared `X / 6 SAFE` forecast are the full live HUD.
- **Hierarchy:** board first; shared forecast and clock second; player names directly on owned tiles third. There is no market rail or instruction wall competing for board space.
- **Motion:** physical slabs lift and travel during the 1.4s fold, then settle before controls matter. Couriers advance on six exact one-second beats. A failed courier tips and drifts beyond the road while retaining its sprite.
- **Phone:** the owned isometric road dominates the middle of the screen. One full-width Turn road control sits in the thumb zone. Copy stays to a single short instruction and never reveals whether that player's road is correct.
- **Reduced motion:** folds and travel snap at their state boundaries, failure becomes a static tipped sprite, and all timing and route information remains visible without motion.
