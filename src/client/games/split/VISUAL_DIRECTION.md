# Split visual direction

**Idea:** a brutal graphic broadcast frame under pressure. The camera border is both arena and antagonist, so it is always the strongest object on the projector.

- **Palette:** near-black field (`#090B0D`), warm cream frame/type (`#F7EFDA`), hazard amber (`#F1B24A`), cut coral (`#FF6B57`), plus the shell's ten seat colours. Cream tethers answer “who is still together?”; coral is reserved for threatened space and resolved cuts.
- **Identity and silhouette:** each survivor carries seat colour, a large seat number, persistent name, and one of circle/triangle/square silhouettes. Edge players retain their numbered colour chip on one of eight discrete border sections.
- **Type and hierarchy:** heavy local Archivo/system sans. Countdown or outcome is the largest text; the frame and threatened band come next; names/tethers follow; clock and frame percentage are peripheral broadcast data.
- **Texture:** a low-contrast registration grid gives the black field print/broadcast character without competing with bodies or tethers. Hard rectangles and square particles keep the visual grammar blunt and scalable.
- **Motion:** cuts use an armed coral wash, countdown beats, bounded square debris, a brief frame flash, then camera zoom as the world-space frame ratchets smaller. Edge attacks show a 0.72-second lane strip, advancing lethal cutoff and inward arrows before resolution. At most two may be active, with starts spaced by 0.8 seconds.
- **Reduced motion:** particles are removed, flash intensity is reduced, and the edge lane plus final lethal cutoff are shown statically. State changes continue through colour, thickness, labels and countdowns rather than shake or rapid flashing.
- **Native rendering:** canvas and CSS are the production assets. They preserve exact border geometry, seat tinting, arbitrary projector/phone scaling and reduced-motion variants better than raster art; no decorative bitmap has a gameplay job in this direction.
