# Log Runner visual direction

**Idea:** a 1960s park-safety field guide thrown into a rushing river. The game begins as an honest geometric grey box: silhouettes, lanes, labels, timing and identity must work before decoration earns space.

- **Palette:** deep river navy (`#082F3A`), foam cream (`#FFF3D1`), bark umber (`#6B3E26`), safety orange (`#FF7A3D`), reed green (`#6FA35B`), plus the shell's ten seat colours. Orange is reserved for incoming danger and response timing.
- **Identity and silhouette:** each runner carries a persistent seat number, short name, seat colour, and one of ten badge patterns. Running, jumping, ducking, wobbling and bank poses have different outer contours at projector scale.
- **Threat language:** low roots occupy the feet lane and say `JUMP`; high branches occupy the head lane and say `DUCK`; fakes are striped hollow silhouettes that peel upward into harmless spray before the response line. Geometry and text duplicate colour information.
- **Type and hierarchy:** heavy local Archivo/system sans. The response verb and final countdown are largest, timer second, runner labels third. No remote fonts.
- **Texture:** code-native bark rings, paper-grain dashes, reeds and bounded foam flecks. Texture never crosses the threat lane or name tags.
- **Motion:** constant-speed right-to-left approach; rolling bark bands; short anticipation squash and eased recovery on actions; bounded splash particles on elimination; an attributed branch arc from bank to lane; gentle result-card settle. Camera shake is limited to a small cluster wipe and never changes the response line.
- **Reduced motion:** disables shake, parallax, particles and pose overshoot. Obstacles still move at gameplay speed because position is the mechanic; a static distance bar and large verb preserve timing clarity.
- **Native rendering:** canvas and CSS are the production assets. They preserve sharp lane geometry, seat tinting, text, arbitrary projector scaling and reduced-motion behavior. Raster concept art is intentionally deferred until the phrase-language playtest proves that more decoration would help rather than obscure it.
